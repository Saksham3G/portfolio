What I work with

Most of the data problems I’ve worked on didn’t start as data problems. They started with someone not trusting a number.

At Accenture I spent six months building a serverless pipeline on AWS — files landing in an S3 bucket, records streaming through Kinesis, Python functions reshaping them on the way to a curated layer. The part I’d defend in an interview isn’t the architecture. It’s the reconciliation SQL underneath it, and the Glue crawlers that kept schemas current when the client’s source formats drifted. Catching duplicates and schema changes before they reach a report is the difference between a dashboard people act on and one they quietly stop opening.

That thread runs through the rest of it. At ATI Travel I read booking data for demand patterns, then cut the time it took to answer the next question. At Traverse India I built Power BI dashboards and learned fast that a dashboard nobody opens is worse than no dashboard at all. At Zeproc I audited fifty product pages for indexing gaps and shipped the fixes — a thirty percent lift in click-through, and a reminder that analysis is worth nothing until something changes because of it.

I’m comfortable at both ends of this. I can write the Lambda function in the morning and sit with a non-technical client team in the afternoon to explain, without jargon, what it does and what I’d do about what it found. Most of my internships needed exactly that, on the same day.
